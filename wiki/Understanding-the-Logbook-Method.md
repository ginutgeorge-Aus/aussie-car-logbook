# Understanding the Logbook Method

The logbook method is the ATO's way of working out **how much of your car use
is for work**, so you can claim that share of your car costs. This app is
built around it. Here it is in plain English.

> ⚠️ This explains how the app works and the general idea of the ATO logbook
> method. It is **not tax advice** — the ATO rules are the final word, and a
> registered tax agent can advise on your situation.

---

## The one number that matters: business-use %

Everything comes down to a single percentage:

> **Business-use % = business kilometres ÷ total kilometres**

If you drove 10,000 km in a year and 7,000 of them were for work, your
business-use % is **70%**. That means you can claim **70%** of your car's
running costs and depreciation.

In the app, every trip you log has a **Business** tick. The app adds up your
business km and your total km, and shows the percentage for you.

---

## The 12-week logbook

The ATO doesn't make you log *every* trip *forever*. Instead:

- You keep a **continuous logbook for at least 12 weeks**.
- Those 12 weeks must be **representative** of your normal driving.
- The business-use % from those 12 weeks can then be used for **up to 5
  years** — as long as your driving pattern doesn't change much.
- You start a new 12-week logbook if things change (new job, new car, big
  change in how you use the car), or when the 5 years are up.

**How this app helps:** log your trips during your 12-week period (odometer
start/end, and tick which are business). The app calculates the business-use %
straight from those trips. Keep your logbook honest and complete — the
odometer readings are your evidence.

> 💡 You still record your **total** kilometres for the full year (usually
> odometer at 1 July and 30 June), because your *claim* applies the percentage
> to the whole year's costs.

---

## What you can claim (the two buckets)

Once you have your business-use %, it's applied to:

### 1. Running costs
Your day-to-day car costs: **fuel, registration, insurance, servicing,
repairs, tyres, lease or interest payments**, and so on. You record these as
expenses in the app.

### 2. Depreciation (decline in value)
Cars lose value over time, and you can claim the business share of that drop.
The app works this out automatically **if** you entered your car's **purchase
date and cost**. A few notes:

- It uses the ATO's **diminishing value** method over an 8-year life.
- The ATO sets a **car cost limit** each year — the most of the car's value
  you can depreciate. The app applies this cap.
- If your reports show a note about depreciation being left out for a year,
  it means that year's cost limit hasn't been set in the app yet — see
  [Troubleshooting](Troubleshooting).

---

## A worked example

- Business-use %: **70%**
- Year's running costs (fuel, rego, insurance, service): **$6,000**
- Car's depreciation for the year: **$4,000**

Your **income-tax deduction** ≈ 70% × ($6,000 + $4,000) = **$7,000**.

(If you're GST-registered, GST is handled separately through your BAS — see
[Reports Explained](Reports-Explained).)

## Keep your records

The ATO expects you to keep your logbook, odometer readings, and receipts.
This app stores them in your own private copy, and the **Print** button on the
Reports page gives you a clean summary to keep or hand to your accountant.
